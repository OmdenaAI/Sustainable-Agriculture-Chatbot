It involves growing two or more crop species (or cultivars) simultaneously on the same piece of land in a variable order (with or without definite rows), providing advantages such as pest and disease control, increased productivity and food security [55,56]. Where the different crops are established following a strict pattern or arrangement, this practice is referred to as intercropping. Recent studies have shown that mixed systems result in higher yields than pure stands because a companion crop usually grows better in scenarios in which the weather conditions do not favor one crop.
