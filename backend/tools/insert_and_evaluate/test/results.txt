Phase 1:

I ran evaluation of our Qdrant index using a QA dataset that I generated using every chunks.json files. To check out my branch -> https://github.com/OmdenaAI/Sustainable-Agriculture-Chatbot/tree/feature/ws2-retrieval-evaluation
Here are the key metrics and some takeaways:
nDCG: 0.2376
MRR:  0.2175
Best Match Score: 0.8595
Average F1:       0.8392
Precision:        0.8229
Recall:           0.8569
Interpretation:
:white_tick: High semantic quality:
The retrieved contexts are semantically well-aligned with the ground truth (see high BERT-based scores like Best Match, F1, Precision, Recall).
This means we're generally retrieving good content.
:warning: Ranking needs improvement:
Low nDCG and MRR suggest that the most relevant passages are not consistently ranked near the top.
So while the info is there, the model isn’t surfacing it effectively upfront => on average, the best answer is ranked fifth from the top.
At the same time, nDCG and MRR@5 is pretty strict, so the fact that the best answer appears at all is a good signal.
Conclusion:
For a lot of questions, unless they're super specific, ground truth is more flexible than just exact paragraph used for the question generation. I would not sweat too much trying to improve ranking because semantic similarity shows that what we retrieve is already relevant.

Phase 2:
I experimented with keyword extraction via KeyBert and spacy's stop words to see whether we can refine Qdrant search by only using the most important words in the query. 
E.g. "How can I reduce waste and minimize my environmental impact while still getting a good yield?" -> "reduce waste minimize environmental impact yield"

Expectations: better hits.
Reality:
Averaged over 801 entries:
ncdg: 0.1809
mrr: 0.1605
best_match_score: 0.8546
average_f1: 0.8326
average_precision: 0.8177
average_recall: 0.8491

Keyword filtering weakened the embeddings by removing useful structure and context. 
This misaligned the vector representation of the query, leading to worse ranking.
